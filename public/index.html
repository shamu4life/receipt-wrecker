Receipt Wrecker

GitHub

Your words as big text, sideways text or Han tiling, or a picture as glyph-art (from a link or a file), for a streamer whose receipt printer runs SassyTP's printer-bot. Paste the result into Twitch chat as a cheer and it prints on the receipt.

Blocks: stacked top to bottom on the receipt
Add text and pictures; they print in order down the receipt. Reorder with ↑/↓, remove with ×. Too much for one receipt just tapes into more, and each is its own cheer (see the bits total under the preview).
Presets: save this whole stack and bring it back
Saved in this browser only. Export the JSON to move a setup to another device, or to survive a cache clear. An uploaded picture's link dies after 15 minutes, so a preset that used one loads with that block flagged for re-upload. A pasted link is left alone.
Starts each message with "Cheer<bits> " so it triggers the print. Turn it off to send a free test: a message with no cheer never reaches the printer, but it does go through chat's filters, so it shows whether the channel lets this markup through before you spend bits on it.
How many bits each cheer spends (1 or more). Each taped part is its own cheer, so a run of parts costs bits × parts; the total is under the preview.
Puts two rotating digits after the cheer ("Cheer100 07"). Twitch won't send the same message twice in a row within 30 seconds (it just isn't sent, and no bits are spent), and the digits make each copy different. They also print on the receipt, which is why this is off. Leave it off and wait 30 seconds before sending the same thing again.
The streamer's printer-bot settings
You can't see these from chat: ask the streamer, or leave the defaults (SassyTP's own). They decide how big a cheer may print.
A cheer of at least this many bits is a High Roller cheer, and the bot prints its big text, sideways text and glyph-art. Below it the bot prints plain text, so every block prints as Han tiling. 0 = High Roller off (every cheer prints as plain text).
How long a High Roller message may print. With both at 0 (the default) it gets the bot's 1600px box, about 42 cm. Bits per inch gives a cheer one inch of tape per that many bits; a maximum length caps it (up to 40 inches). The bot fades out whatever runs past either limit, and this app splits a tall stack into more cheers so nothing is cut.
The preview is drawn by SassyTP's printer-bot renderer (MIT), the receipt page the bot itself prints from, with a grey box where the Twitch logo goes. The thermal view is what the printer gets, give or take fonts: this computer's fonts may differ from the streamer's. It is drawn one pixel per printer dot, so it shows larger than the preview. Big and sideways text land within a dot or two of where the bot puts them; small character grids on 58 mm, and Braille, can land up to 3 dots off, so their texture here is approximate. Pick the dither the streamer's dock is set to (the Dither menu works while the thermal view is on).
Two one-cheer tests of the streamer's settings. High Roller test cheers exactly the threshold: if BIG prints big, High Roller is on at that amount. Plain test cheers one bit under it: it should print small, inside quote marks, with every line the same length.

Paste into the official Twitch web or mobile chat and send it as a cheer. Only the first-party client actually cheers.

Held or blocked by chat? That is the channel's moderation saying no, so don't rework the message to get it past. A free test (Cheer-ready off) shows what chat lets through before you spend bits; if the channel blocks the big-text markup, Han tiling, which is plain text, is the fallback. If chat held it for its words, don't send those words again in another form, Han tiling included: that is the moderators saying no to the words.
