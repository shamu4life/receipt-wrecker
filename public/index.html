Receipt Wrecker

GitHub

Your words as big text, sideways text or Han tiling, or a picture as glyph-art (from a link or a file), for a streamer whose receipt printer runs SassyTP's printer-bot. Paste the result into Twitch chat as a cheer and it prints on the receipt.

Blocks: stacked top to bottom on the receipt
Add text and pictures; they print in order down the receipt. Reorder with ↑/↓, remove with × (Undo brings a removed block back). Too much for one receipt just tapes into more, and each is its own cheer (the cost is under the preview).
The preview is drawn by SassyTP's printer-bot renderer (MIT), in this computer's fonts, which may differ from the streamer's.
What's this?
The preview is the receipt page the bot itself prints from, and the thermal view is what the printer gets, give or take fonts. The grey box in the footer is where the Twitch logo goes. The thermal view is drawn one pixel per printer dot, so it shows larger than the preview, and then scaled to fit the column: on a narrow window, or a screen that isn't high-density, some rows and columns of dots are skipped on screen. Zoom the page in to see every dot. Big and sideways text and plain Han tiling come out within a dot or two of the bot's. Small character grids on 58 mm, and Braille, do not: their thin strokes and texture here can differ from the print (in Crisp a stroke can vanish here and still print), so judge those with the thermal view off. Pick the dither the streamer's dock is set to (the Thermal dither menu works while the thermal view is on). A cheer for the streamer's Letter printer shows its page at the printer's 300 dots to the inch, scaled to the column: in black and white the bot prints text sharp and shades only pictures, and in colour it prints the page as it looks, with no dots.
Two one-cheer tests of the streamer's settings. High Roller test cheers exactly the threshold: if BIG prints big, High Roller is on at that amount. Plain test cheers one bit under it (1 bit when High Roller is off or the printer doesn't allow High Roller prints): it should print small, inside quote marks, with every line the same length.
Presets: save this whole stack and bring it back
Saved in this browser only. Export the JSON to move a setup to another device, or to survive a cache clear. An uploaded picture's link dies after 15 minutes, so a preset that used one loads with that block flagged: pick the file again or paste a fresh link. Import never replaces a saved setup: one whose name is taken is added with a number after it.
Adds "Cheer<bits>" to each message (at the start, or at the end of a plain-text part) so it triggers the print. Turn it off to send a free test: a message with no cheer never reaches the printer, but it does go through chat's filters, so it shows whether the channel lets this markup through before you spend bits on it.
How many bits each cheer spends (1 or more). Each taped part is its own cheer, so a run of parts costs bits × parts; the total is under the preview.
Puts two rotating digits after the cheer ("Cheer100 07"). Twitch won't send the same message twice in a row within 30 seconds (it just isn't sent, and no bits are spent), and the digits make each copy different. They also print on the receipt, which is why it is off by default. Without it, wait 30 seconds before sending the same thing again.
The streamer's printer-bot settings
You can't see these from chat: ask the streamer, or leave the defaults (SassyTP's own). They decide how big a cheer may print.
A cheer of at least this many bits is a High Roller cheer, and the bot prints its big text, sideways text and glyph-art. Below it the bot prints plain text, so every block prints in its plain form (big and sideways text as Han tiling). 0 = High Roller off (every cheer prints as plain text).
How long a High Roller message may print. With both at 0 (the default) it gets the bot's longest message, about 42 cm. Bits per inch gives a cheer one inch of tape per that many bits; a maximum length caps it (up to 40 inches). The bot fades out whatever runs past either limit, and this app splits a tall stack into more cheers so nothing is cut.
Printer Bot 3.0 has this switch for each printer, on by default. When it is off, that printer prints every cheer as plain text, however many bits, so every block here prints in its plain form. Printer Bot 2.5 has no such switch: leave it on. With a Letter printer (below), this is the roll printer's switch; the app takes the Letter printer's as on.
Pictures from links (Printer Bot 3.0):
The sites the streamer switched on in Printer Bot's dock, under Advanced, Images in messages (every site starts off). A Real picture then prints from a direct link on one of them, in a High Roller cheer, if the printer also has Allow images on (its default). Printer Bot 2.5 prints no pictures from links.
A Letter printer (Printer Bot 3.0)
For a streamer with a second printer, on Letter paper, that big cheers go to: the amount its rule starts at, as on its card in the dock (for example Bits ≥ 500). Every smaller cheer prints on the roll. 0 = no Letter printer. A cheer on Letter is built for its page: 7.5 inches wide, one page a cheer.

What's this?
The Letter printer's card in the dock. Scaled like 80 mm prints the roll's receipt on the page, larger, which leaves a message about 6 cm under the profile picture. Large message gives the message most of the page, about 16 cm, and lays it out from the left, so a block here centres itself. Image fit is how a picture from a link fills the space the message leaves it in Large message: Fit shows all of it, Fill covers the space and crops the rest, Stretch draws it to the space. In Colour, Big text, Sideways text and Glyph-art can have colours (their cards show the pickers); Black and white prints text sharp and shades only pictures. The app takes the printer's Allow High Roller prints and Allow images as on, their defaults.

Paste into the official Twitch web or mobile chat and send it as a cheer. Only the first-party client actually cheers.

Held or blocked by chat? That is the channel's moderation saying no, so don't rework the message to get it past. A free test (Cheer-ready off) shows what chat lets through before you spend bits; if the channel blocks the big-text markup, Han tiling, which is plain text, is the fallback. If chat held it for its words, don't send those words again in another form, Han tiling included: that is the moderators saying no to the words.
